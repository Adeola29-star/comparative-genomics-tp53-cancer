# Comparative Genomics of TP53 Across Vertebrates

## Project Overview

TP53 is one of the most important tumour suppressor genes in humans. It plays essential roles in maintaining genomic stability through regulation of DNA damage response, cell-cycle control, and apoptosis.

Mutations affecting TP53 are among the most common genetic alterations observed in human cancers, making it a key gene for understanding tumour development and cancer suppression.

This project applies comparative genomics approaches to investigate how TP53 has evolved across vertebrates and how its evolutionary conservation provides insight into its importance in cancer biology.

---

## Biological Question

**How has TP53 evolved across vertebrates, and what does its conservation reveal about cancer suppression?**

---

## Objectives

The objectives of this project were to:

- Compare TP53 protein sequences from representative vertebrate species.
- Investigate sequence similarity and evolutionary relationships among TP53 proteins.
- Examine conserved and variable sequence patterns across species.
- Construct a phylogenetic tree to visualise TP53 evolutionary relationships.
- Interpret how TP53 conservation relates to its essential role as a tumour suppressor.

---

## Species Included

The following vertebrate species were selected for comparison:

- Human (*Homo sapiens*)
- Chimpanzee (*Pan troglodytes*)
- Mouse (*Mus musculus*)
- African elephant (*Loxodonta africana*)
- Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*)

These species represent different evolutionary distances, allowing investigation of TP53 conservation across vertebrate lineages.

---

## Workflow

1. TP53 protein sequences were retrieved from public biological databases including UniProt and NCBI.

2. Multiple sequence alignment was performed using **Clustal Omega** to compare amino acid similarities and differences between species.

3. A pairwise percentage identity matrix was generated to measure sequence similarity between TP53 proteins.

4. A phylogenetic tree was constructed to examine evolutionary relationships among the selected species.

5. Sequence similarities and differences observed in the alignment were interpreted in the context of TP53 evolution and cancer suppression.

## Key Findings

- TP53 showed strong conservation across the vertebrate species analysed.
- Human and chimpanzee TP53 sequences showed the highest similarity in this dataset.
- Mouse and African elephant TP53 proteins retained high similarity to human TP53.
- Zebrafish TP53 showed greater evolutionary divergence compared with mammalian species.
- The overall conservation of TP53 across vertebrates suggests strong evolutionary pressure to maintain its tumour suppressor function.

---

## Biological Interpretation

The conservation of TP53 throughout vertebrate evolution highlights its importance in maintaining cellular stability.

Because essential genes are often preserved through evolution, regions of TP53 that remain similar between species are likely to contribute to important biological functions.

In cancer biology, this evolutionary conservation provides insight into why alterations affecting critical regions of TP53 can have significant consequences. Mutations that disrupt TP53 function may impair processes such as DNA damage response, cell-cycle regulation, and apoptosis, contributing to tumour development.

Therefore, comparative genomics provides an evolutionary perspective for understanding the biological importance of TP53 and its role in cancer suppression.

---

## Relevance of African Elephant TP53

African elephants were included because of their unique relevance to cancer biology through **Peto's paradox**.

Despite having a much larger number of cells than humans, elephants have lower-than-expected cancer rates. Research has suggested that enhanced tumour suppression mechanisms, including TP53-related pathways, may contribute to this observation.

Although this project does not investigate elephant cancer resistance directly, comparing elephant TP53 with other vertebrates provides an evolutionary context for understanding how tumour suppressor mechanisms may vary across species.
